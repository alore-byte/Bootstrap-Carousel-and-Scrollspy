Section 1

Space

Our solar system and the wider universe are filled with cosmic wonders, from scorching planets to colossal structures of dust and gas. Venus, the second planet from the Sun, is a rocky world experiencing a runaway greenhouse effect, making its surface hot enough to melt lead. Further out spins Jupiter, a massive gas giant so large that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it, famous for its centuries-old storm known as the Great Red Spot. Beyond our planetary neighborhood floats the Orion Nebula, a vast interstellar cloud of dust, hydrogen, and helium gas that acts as a stellar nursery where new stars are actively being born. Even further in deep space lies Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole located at the very center of our Milky Way galaxy, possessing a gravitational pull so intense that not even light can escape its boundary.

Section 2

Nature

Nature is full of astonishing connections and adaptations, such as trees communicating and sharing nutrients underground through a vast network of fungi, dolphins using unique signature whistles to call and identify one another, and sharks having roamed the oceans for over 400 million years, pre-dating dinosaurs. Additionally, honey bees communicate food locations through a specialized directional "waggle" dance, African buffalo females use a democratic-style glance voting system to decide travel directions, and massive blue whales can grow up to 200 tons while producing heartbeats audible from miles away. Learn more about global wildlife and conservation programs via the World Wildlife Fund Fact Collection.
